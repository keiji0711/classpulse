# ClassPulse Pitch Deck and Business Model Guide

**Document purpose:** A slide-by-slide guide for presenting ClassPulse to schools, partners, investors, or competition judges.  
**Business model reflected here:** Schools use the platform without a subscription fee. ClassPulse earns through configurable monthly parent-access fees.  
**Prepared:** August 2026

---

## 1. The pitch in one sentence

> ClassPulse is a connected school operations and parent-engagement platform that gives schools free digital tools while families pay an affordable monthly fee for secure, timely access to their learner's school information.

## 2. The 30-second elevator pitch

Schools often manage attendance, grades, examinations, parent messages, and year-end records through disconnected spreadsheets, paper, and chat applications. This slows teachers down and leaves parents waiting for reliable updates.

ClassPulse connects the entire flow. School administrators organize academic operations on the web, teachers record verified classroom information, and authorized parents see attendance, grades, exam scores, and school updates through a secure mobile app.

Schools pay no subscription fee. Revenue comes from an affordable monthly parent-access fee, creating a low-friction path for schools to adopt the platform while ClassPulse grows with the number of families served.

## Optional Slide 6A — Faster, safer roster onboarding

Use this slide when pitching schools, implementation partners, or judges who want to see a concrete workflow advantage.

### On the slide

**From an existing DepEd SF1 to a verified digital class roster**

1. Adviser selects an assigned section.
2. Adviser uploads the existing `.xls` or `.xlsx` SF1 workbook.
3. ClassPulse extracts learner LRNs, names, and parent choices into an editable review screen.
4. The system verifies the DepEd School ID, active school year, grade level, and section.
5. The adviser selects the father or mother as guardian, reviews exceptions, and confirms once.

**Built-in safeguards**

- Import is blocked when the school, year, grade, or section does not match.
- Database authorization confirms the instructor is the assigned adviser.
- Duplicate LRNs and existing enrollments are identified before completion.
- Missing contact numbers remain clearly marked for later completion.
- The original workbook is read for extraction and is not stored by ClassPulse.

### Suggested visual

Use a left-to-right flow: `DepEd SF1 → Four identity checks → Review table → Verified class roster`. Add four green check cards labeled School ID, School Year, Grade, and Section.

### Speaker notes

“Digital transformation should not force teachers to encode an existing roster again. ClassPulse reuses the school’s SF1, but it does not trust the filename alone. The browser and database independently confirm that the form belongs to the correct school, current year, grade, and adviser-assigned section before any learner is added.”

## Slide 13 — Revenue mechanics

### On the slide

**Core formulas**

```text
Expected monthly revenue
= Eligible learners × Monthly parent fee

Collected revenue
= Sum of payments marked paid

Verified revenue
= Sum of paid amounts with verified remittance

Collection rate
= Paid learners ÷ Eligible learners × 100

Annual recurring revenue run rate
= Verified monthly revenue × 12
```

| 1,000 | 800 | ₱16,000 | ₱192,000 |
| 5,000 | 4,000 | ₱80,000 | ₱960,000 |

# Part II — Business model

## 1. Customer and user map

| Stakeholder | Role | Receives value | Pays? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform owner | Operates ClassPulse | School network, support, security, revenue visibility | No |
| School administrator | Organizational buyer/champion | Free operations platform and consolidated reporting | No subscription fee |
| Teacher | Daily operational user | Faster class workflows and clearer records | No |
| Parent/guardian | Mobile end user | Secure, convenient access to learner information | Monthly access fee |
| Learner | Beneficiary | Better visibility, follow-up, and family engagement | No direct payment |

The **school is the distribution partner and operational customer**, while the **parent is the paying user**.

## 2. Value propositions

### For schools

- Digitize core workflows without a software subscription fee.
- Reuse existing DepEd SF1 rosters through a verified review-and-import workflow.
- Consolidate school records and reporting.
- Improve parent communication and transparency.
- Preserve academic-year history.
- Monitor parent-access collections.

### For teachers

- Use assigned schedules and rosters instead of rebuilding lists.
- Import an assigned class from SF1 while reviewing names, guardians, and missing contacts before saving.
- Record attendance in a validated workflow.
- Enter grades and examinations in the same platform.
- Communicate through structured school channels.

### For parents

- See relevant information without waiting for paper reports.
- Access attendance, grades, exams, and messages in one place.
- Receive information tied to the correct learner and school.
- Use secure PIN, child switching, and account controls.

### For ClassPulse

- Lower school-acquisition friction through free institutional access.
- Build recurring revenue from parent access.
- Grow revenue as school and parent adoption increases.
- Use verified collection data to measure real revenue rather than promises.

## 3. Revenue model

### Primary model — Monthly parent access

Each school has a configurable monthly price per eligible learner. Parent access for a billing month is determined by the configured rules, grace period, and recorded payment or waiver.

```text
Gross verified revenue = Σ verified parent payments
```

Do not describe “collected” money as final revenue until it is verified according to the remittance process.

### Recommended pricing experiment

Do not lock pricing before pilot evidence. Test price and willingness to pay.

| Test | Monthly price | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Accessibility test | ₱10–₱20 | Maximize parent activation and establish habit |
| Base-value test | ₱20–₱30 | Test sustainable recurring access pricing |
| Enhanced-service test | ₱40–₱50 | Only after adding clear extra parent value |

The application currently supports a configurable amount per school. **₱20 is a useful base-case assumption for pitch calculations, not a final commitment.**

## 4. Alternative business models to consider

These are strategic options, not all current product features.

### Model A — 100% parent-access revenue

ClassPulse retains verified access payments and covers platform operations.

**Advantages:** Simple revenue story and highest gross margin potential.  
**Risks:** Schools may need a clear reason to administer collections without compensation.

### Model B — Revenue share with the school

A defined portion supports the school or its implementation activities.

**Advantages:** Stronger school incentive and possible faster distribution.  
**Risks:** Lower ClassPulse revenue, more reconciliation, and contractual complexity.

### Model C — Collector incentive

A small fixed amount or percentage rewards authorized personnel for collection and remittance work.

**Advantages:** Clear operational incentive.  
**Risks:** Cash-control, payroll, tax, and accountability questions.

### Model D — Sponsored access

Local government, NGOs, alumni, or corporate partners cover parent access for selected schools or families.

**Advantages:** Improves inclusion and enables public-school adoption.  
**Risks:** Longer sales cycle and dependence on sponsor renewal.

### Model E — Optional paid services for institutions

The core school platform remains free, but schools may purchase optional implementation services such as migration, custom training, premium support, or integrations.

**Advantages:** Adds revenue without restoring a required school subscription.  
**Risks:** Services are less scalable than software revenue.

### Recommended starting combination

Use **Model A or C for the pilot**, add **Model D** for affordability, and consider **Model E** only when onboarding demand becomes operationally expensive. Do not promise a revenue share until the legal, tax, cash-handling, and reconciliation rules are documented.

## 6. Unit economics worksheet

Replace the bracketed values with pilot data.

```text
Eligible learners per school             = [A]
Monthly fee per learner                   = [B]
Parent payment rate                       = [C%]
Gross verified revenue per school/month   = A × B × C

Less collection/revenue-share cost        = [D]
Less payment-processing cost              = [E]
Less allocated hosting/support cost       = [F]

Contribution margin per school/month      = (A × B × C) − D − E − F

School acquisition cost                   = [G]
Payback period in months                   = G ÷ contribution margin
```

### Example only

For 1,000 eligible learners, a ₱20 monthly fee, and 80% payment conversion:

```text
Monthly gross verified revenue = 1,000 × ₱20 × 80%
                               = ₱16,000
```

This is not profit. Deduct incentives, payment fees, support, hosting, taxes, and other operating costs.

## 7. Key business risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Parents do not see enough value | Prioritize timely attendance, grades, exams, and trusted school updates |
| Cash collection is difficult to reconcile | Require collector identity, monthly records, remittance status, and platform verification |
| Low-income families cannot pay | Grace periods, controlled waivers, and sponsored-access programs |
| Teachers do not consistently use the system | Reduce steps, train champions, and monitor workflow completion |
| School adoption stalls | Keep platform access free and run a limited, supported pilot |
| Privacy concerns | Clear consent, minimum data exposure, role boundaries, MFA, and audit trails |
| Revenue depends on one feature | Continue improving the overall parent experience and retention value |
| Collections could create reputational issues | Publish transparent policies for pricing, waivers, access, refunds, and support |

## 8. Policy documents needed before scaling

- Parent access terms and pricing disclosure
- School participation agreement
- Data-processing and privacy agreement
- Collector authorization and cash-handling procedure
- Remittance and verification policy
- Waiver and sponsored-access policy
- Refund and dispute policy
- Parent support and account-recovery procedure
- School offboarding and data-retention procedure

# Part V — Questions you should be ready to answer

## Product

**What happens if the internet is unavailable?**  
Explain the current connectivity requirements honestly and describe any planned offline support only as a roadmap item.

**Can schools export their data?**  
Yes. Authorized users have structured export capabilities for relevant records.

**Can previous academic years be changed?**  
Historical years are designed to remain readable and protected from normal operational edits.

**Who can see learner data?**  
Access is based on authenticated role, school, assignment, and parent-to-learner relationship.

**How do you ensure an uploaded SF1 belongs to the correct class?**  
Each school has a unique six-digit DepEd School ID. Before import, ClassPulse matches the workbook’s School ID, school year, grade, and section against the logged-in instructor’s school, the current active academic year, and an adviser-assigned section. The interface blocks mismatches, and the database repeats the same checks before writing any records.

**What happens when an SF1 contains duplicate or previously enrolled learners?**  
The review identifies invalid or duplicate LRNs. Existing school learners can be enrolled into the current year when appropriate, while learners already enrolled in that year are skipped rather than silently duplicated.

## Business

**Why will schools promote something they do not earn from?**  
Because they receive the operating platform without a subscription fee. If pilots show that collection effort requires an incentive, ClassPulse can evaluate a documented collector or school-share model.

**Why will parents pay?**  
The hypothesis is that parents will pay a small recurring amount for trusted, convenient, and timely access to attendance, academic results, and school communication. This must be validated through pilot activation, conversion, and retention data.

**What if a parent cannot pay?**  
The platform supports waivers and grace periods. Sponsored access should be explored for inclusion.

**Is collected cash automatically revenue?**  
No. The operating model distinguishes collected payments from verified remittance. Use verified payments for revenue reporting.

**What is the final monthly price?**  
It is configurable per school. Use ₱20 only as the current pitch baseline until willingness-to-pay and cost data support a final price.

## Scale

**How does ClassPulse make more money?**  
By onboarding more schools, activating more eligible families, improving parent payment conversion, and retaining monthly parent usage—while keeping collection and service costs controlled.

**What is the defensible advantage?**  
ClassPulse combines school workflows, protected academic history, teacher-assignment boundaries, parent communication, and parent-revenue operations in one connected system designed for Philippine K–12 use.
